Offer design comes first

Great design can’t rescue a weak offer.

Most collateral fails before anyone opens a design file. It lists features, buries the price and leaves the buyer to work out whether it’s worth the risk. So we start with the offer itself: what the customer gets, what it costs them, how fast they see a return and what happens if it doesn’t work.

We use the value equation popularized by Alex Hormozi as the checklist. Raise the outcome and the buyer’s confidence in it; shrink the wait and the effort. The materials then simply make each lever visible.

Value = Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood Time delay × Effort & sacrifice
Offer leverHow the Arobella materials pulled it

A no-obligation two-week trial turned a capital decision into a low-stakes test.

↔ Fit Ways to buy

Three acquisition paths — outright purchase, unlimited-use rental and pay-per-minute — so budget structure never became the reason to say no.

Case study · Medical device · Arobella Medical

A hospital value dossier for an ultrasound wound therapy device.

Arobella’s Qoustic Wound Therapy System is an FDA-cleared, 35 kHz ultrasound device for wound debridement. The clinical results were strong. The problem was the sale: a $45,000+ capital purchase that clinicians liked but administrators had to justify. We built one document that answered every member of the buying committee.

12 page dossier written for the whole buying committee
6 buyer ROI and monthly-net scenarios modeled
15+ CPT billing codes mapped with Medicare rates
3 acquisition paths: buy, rent or pay per minute

What made it work

Written for each reader, in one document Clinicians, administrators, billing staff and value-analysis committees each got a section that answered their question, with a contents page to jump straight there.
Economics in the buyer’s terms Instead of a price list, each scenario showed patients per month, the billing code, the facility payment and the resulting payback — the numbers an administrator takes to a capital committee.
Reimbursement made concrete A full coding and payment reference turned “will we get paid for this?” from an objection into a selling point.
Proof the buyer can check Case timelines, institutional users and a referenced bibliography gave the clinical champion something credible to circulate internally.

Each scenario used a single billing code and a realistic monthly patient volume, so a burn unit, a wound clinic and a surgical team could each find their own case.

Lead generation

Materials that start conversations, not just close them.

A brochure that only reaches people already talking to sales is half a tool. We split the content into assets that do a job at each stage, so prospects raise their hand, tell you who they are and arrive at the first call already halfway convinced.

  1. 1 Attract

    A useful, ungated piece that earns attention from the right buyer — a reimbursement quick-reference, a short explainer video or a benchmark.

    Arobella example · The coding & payment guide works on its own for billing teams.

  2. 2 Capture

    A higher-value asset offered in exchange for contact details and a few qualifying questions, routed straight into your CRM.

    Arobella example · The facility ROI model and competitor comparison were offered on request.

  3. 3 Nurture

    A short sequence of case studies and proof points that lets the champion build the internal case before a rep ever calls.

    Arobella example · Limb-salvage, graft-bed and biofilm case timelines, one per touch.

  4. 4 Convert

    A low-risk next step that makes saying yes easy, backed by the full dossier tailored to the prospect’s own numbers.

    Arobella example · The no-obligation two-week equipment trial.
