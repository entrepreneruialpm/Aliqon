Structured on PMI’s project charter

Too busy to do the research?

The opportunity is clear. The details aren’t.

Sponsors are the people with the vision and the budget authority — and usually the least time. A new opportunity lands on the desk, everyone agrees it looks promising, and then it sits, because nobody has the hours to research the market, talk to the right people and pin down what it would take.

Project chartering closes that gap. We take the idea from a conversation to a documented charter, following PMI’s chartering process, so you can decide with evidence: commit, reshape, or walk away before real money is spent.

What we explore and uncover

Eight things every new project needs settled.

A charter isn’t paperwork. It’s the research and the hard conversations that turn “we should do this” into a project the team can actually deliver.

01

Background & business case

Why this, and why now?

Desk research on the market, competitors and technology, so the charter rests on evidence rather than a hunch.

02

Benefits & goals

What outcomes need to be delivered?

The benefits you’re after, turned into specific, measurable objectives and the success criteria the project will be judged on.

03

Requirements

What must it do?

High-level requirements gathered from the people who will use, buy, build or approve it — before anyone starts designing.

04

Scope & boundaries

What’s in, and what’s out?

Clear lines around the work, plus the constraints and assumptions everyone is quietly relying on.

05

Stakeholders

Who can make or break it?

A map of everyone with influence or interest — sponsors, partners, customers, regulators — and what each needs to say yes.

06

Risks

What could kill it?

The few high-impact risks that threaten the whole opportunity, ranked, with owners and early tests.

07

Path to launch

What will it take to launch?

Major milestones, dependencies and the people, partners and capabilities needed to get from approval to launch.

08

Preliminary cost

Roughly what will it cost?

An order-of-magnitude budget with its assumptions shown, so you know the size of the commitment before you make it.

Hunt for the fatal flaw

What could kill this opportunity?

Most risk registers list everything and prioritize nothing. We go looking for the few risks that could end the opportunity outright — while there’s still time and money to test them, design around them or pivot.

Each one comes with an owner, an early warning sign and the cheapest way to find out whether it’s real.

Market

Will customers actually buy — at a price that works?

Demand, willingness to pay and the strength of competing alternatives.

Technical

Can it be built, scaled and supported?

Unproven technology, performance gaps and scale-up hurdles.

Regulatory

What approvals stand between you and launch?

Registrations, certifications and compliance that set the timeline.

Financial

Do the economics hold up?

Cost to deliver, margin and how long until the investment pays back.

Organizational

Do we have the people, partners and sponsorship?

Capacity, key partners and whether leadership will stay committed.

How a chartering engagement runs

A little of your time. A lot of ours.

  1. 1

    Sponsor kickoff

    Your time: one conversation

    We capture the idea, the benefit you’re after, what you already know and who we should talk to.

  2. 2

    Research & discovery

    Your time: little to none

    We do the legwork: desk research, stakeholder and customer interviews, and a first pass at requirements, risks and cost.

  3. 3

    Draft charter

    Your time: a review

    We bring the findings together into a draft charter and walk you through the risks and trade-offs we found.

  4. 4

    Decide & hand off

    Your time: a decision

    You approve, reshape or stop. Approved charters hand straight to a project team, or to us to plan and run.
