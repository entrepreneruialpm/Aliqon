Portfolio selection & management

The method

Score the business, not just the science.

Most portfolios are picked on technical merit and the loudest champion. The projects that stall usually stall for business reasons: no partner to manufacture, no buyer who knows it exists, a team that needs cash this quarter.

So each gate asks a harder business question than the last, and each costs more effort than the one before. Cheap screens go wide; deep analysis is saved for the few that earn it.

  1. Gate 1 · Enroll

    Strategic screen

    Does this belong in the portfolio at all?

    A short scorecard on three factors: the strength of the partner network, the balance of capital needed against jobs created, and how innovative the value proposition is. Simple enough for applicants to fill in themselves, so every candidate is compared on the same terms.

    Out: a ranked long list and a database you can trend year to year.

  2. Gate 2 · Evaluate

    Techno-economic review

    Can this become a business, with this team?

    A working review with each company covering markets, competitors, industry structure, team, assets, environmental claims, strategic fit and a discounted cash flow. Concerns are flagged yellow or red rather than averaged away.

    Out: a scored business case and flag list per candidate.

  3. Gate 3 · Commit

    Final selection

    Where does the next dollar do the most good?

    Finalists are compared side by side on impact, readiness and risk. Every candidate, selected or not, leaves with a written summary and a specific next step: grant collaboration, a check-in date or an open door.

    Out: the funded shortlist and a pipeline of “not yet” candidates.

Case study · Agriculture · Bio-based materials

84 soy-based technologies. Four worth backing.

A state soybean commodity board wanted to grow demand for soybeans by backing bio-based products that could reach market within five years. As program manager, Kenny designed the evaluation tools, recruited the technologies and led the evaluation team that took the field from 84 submissions to four finalists.

84 technologies submitted and scored
11 business areas reviewed for each Stage 2 candidate
25+ industry, academic and finance professionals on the team
50+ organizations engaged across the program

What made it work

Recruited the pipeline, not just judged it Press releases, outreach through the innovation center’s network and cold calls to companies found online brought in submissions from seven states and Italy.
Business readiness as a hard gate Anything still at bench scale was set aside before the expensive analysis began, which kept the deep review for technologies already near market entry.
Flags instead of averages Single-person dependencies, weak IP protection and low market awareness were called out as red or yellow flags, so a high overall score couldn’t hide a serious risk.
Demand evidence alongside the scores A parallel state-funded material flow study, including homeowner and architect surveys, sized how much petroleum-based product the soy alternatives could realistically replace.

After the shortlist

Selection is one decision. Management is every one after it.

Three of the seven Stage 2 candidates dropped out, and not because the science was weak. All three were based out of state, two needed cash immediately and one wasn’t sure the program was worth its time. A good portfolio process catches that, records why, and keeps the door open for when the timing changes.

How an engagement runs

Agree the yardstick. Run the gates. Hand over the system.

  1. 1

    Agree the yardstick

    We work with sponsors to set the goals the portfolio must serve, then translate them into criteria, weights and gate questions everyone signs off on.

  2. 2

    Run the gates

    We screen the full list, run the deeper reviews on the candidates that earn them and present a ranked shortlist with flags and reasoning.

  3. 3

    Hand over the system

    You get the scorecards, templates and tracker, plus a review calendar, so the next funding cycle runs without starting from scratch.
