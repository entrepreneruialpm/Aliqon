02 — Prove it

It works in the field.
Will it make money at scale?

Techno-economic analysis (TEA) connects the science to the spreadsheet. For biological and agricultural technologies, we build a transparent model of what it costs to bring your product to market at commercial scale, what growers and buyers will pay, and which few assumptions decide whether the business case holds — before anyone commits the capital to find out the hard way.

A short walkthrough of how TEA works and the decisions it supports.

Why it matters

Most business cases don't fail on the science. They fail on the economics.

A promising result in the lab or a trial plot is the start of the question, not the answer. Between that result and a profitable product sit a handful of variables that can quietly turn a great technology into an uncompetitive one.

TEA finds those variables early, puts a number on each one, and shows you exactly how much room you have before the case breaks. That turns "we think this could work" into a position you can defend to a board, an investment committee or a grower association.

Where business cases usually break

  1. Performance beyond the trialEfficacy, yield gains and consistency that slip between the greenhouse, small plots and real farms across seasons and regions.
  2. Cost to produce and deliverSeed production, biological inputs, contract manufacturing and logistics that look cheap until you need them in volume.
  3. Time and capital to marketDevelopment, regulatory and registration timelines that stretch payback past what investors will tolerate.
  4. The incumbent's priceThe product or practice you're replacing is often cheap, familiar and trusted by growers.
  5. Adoption barriersRegulation, switching costs, channel partners and grower habits that slow the revenue curve.
The questions it answers

Clear answers to the questions that decide funding.

COST

What will it cost to make at scale?

A bottom-up cost per acre, dose or unit across production, delivery and go-to-market.

COMPETE

Can it beat the incumbent?

Your cost and performance set against the products buyers already use.

RISK

Which assumptions matter most?

Sensitivity analysis ranks the variables that make or break the case.

TARGET

What has to be true to win?

Break-even points and performance targets, such as yield gain or price premium, your R&D team can aim at.

Questions

Common questions about TEA

How early is too early for a TEA?

Earlier is usually better. A first-pass TEA at the lab or early-trial stage is when it's cheapest to change course, and it tells your R&D team which performance targets matter most. The model is then refined as real data replaces assumptions.

What do you need from us?

Whatever trial, performance and cost data you have, along with access to the people who know the technology. Where data doesn't exist yet, we use documented benchmarks and flag them clearly, so you know which numbers to firm up first.

How is this different from a financial model?

A financial model usually starts with revenue and margin assumptions. A TEA starts with how the technology performs in the field or the market — yield, efficacy, adoption, cost to produce and apply — and builds the economics from the ground up, then tests them against the market. That's what makes it useful for judging technology risk.

Can you work under NDA with investors and founders?

Yes. Confidentiality is standard on every engagement, and in diligence work we can coordinate data access directly with the company on the investor's behalf.

Do we keep the model?

Yes. You receive the working model with every assumption visible and editable, so your team can keep using and updating it after the engagement ends.
