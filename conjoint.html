Conjoint analysis · Pricing evidence

The trade-off truth.

Ask customers what they want and they'll say "everything, and cheaper." Conjoint analysis makes them choose — and those choices reveal which features drive the purchase and exactly what each segment will pay.

The problem

Most pricing is a guess dressed up as a spreadsheet.

Launch prices usually come from cost-plus math, a competitor's list price or a founder's gut. Each one leaves money on the table — or quietly caps adoption.

Typical Direct questions

"How important is battery life?"  → Very important.
"Would you pay more for it?"  → Sure.

Every feature scores high. Stated willingness-to-pay runs well above real behavior. You learn what people say, not what they do.

Conjoint Forced trade-offs

"Product A: longer life, $40 more. Product B: standard life, lower price. Which do you buy?"

Repeated across realistic choice sets, the decisions expose the hidden value of every feature — in dollars — and where demand falls off.

What it reveals

Which features earn their cost — and which don't.

  • Feature importance. How much each attribute actually moves the purchase decision.
  • Willingness to pay, in dollars. The price premium each feature can carry, by segment.
  • Price–volume curve. Where revenue peaks and where demand breaks.
  • Segments that matter. Buyers who look alike on paper but trade off very differently.
  • Competitive share. How your product performs head-to-head against named alternatives.

What you walk away with

A decision, not a data dump.

  1. Design

    We define the decision, the attributes and the price ranges with your team, then build realistic choice sets.

  2. Field

    Survey programming and fielding with the right buyers — growers, clinicians, contractors, procurement — not a generic panel.

  3. Analyze

    Feature importance, willingness-to-pay and segment differences, modeled with established choice-based methods.

  4. Simulate

    A price–volume simulator your team keeps, plus a written recommendation you can defend to your board.

Questions

Common questions.

When is conjoint the right tool?

When a product has several features that can vary, buyers face real alternatives and price is a live decision — launch pricing, repackaging, a new tier or validating a revenue model before investment.

How many respondents do we need?

It depends on the number of attributes and segments. Many B2B studies work with a few hundred qualified buyers; we size the sample during scoping so the results hold up.

How long does a study take?

A typical engagement runs several weeks from scoping to final readout. The longest step is usually reaching the right respondents, and we plan around that from day one.

Can you work with niche or technical buyers?

Yes. Our background is agriculture, life sciences and medical device — markets where the buyer is an agronomist, a superintendent or a hospital administrator, and generic consumer panels don't apply.

Have a price to set — or a price to test?
